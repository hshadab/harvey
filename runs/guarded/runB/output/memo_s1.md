## SECTION I — FINANCIAL AND QUALITY OF EARNINGS

### RF-01 | CRITICAL | Adjusted EBITDA Discrepancy: CIM vs. QoE Data Package

The CIM and the QoE data package submitted to the quality of earnings advisor present materially different Adjusted EBITDA figures for FY2024. The gap is approximately one million dollars, and the QoE data package itself acknowledges that the specific adjustment category causing the discrepancy has not been identified. At the implied EBITDA multiple range, this discrepancy translates to a valuation swing of approximately eight to nine million dollars.

**Action:** Demand a fully reconciled EBITDA bridge with line-item support before finalizing the purchase price. The QoE report must address this gap explicitly. Consider price protection via a post-closing true-up mechanism tied to verified Adjusted EBITDA.

---

### RF-02 | CRITICAL | DOE IDIQ Ceiling Exhaustion — Projected Revenue Cliff in FY2025

The Company's largest customer relationship is a federal IDIQ contract representing approximately 19% of TTM revenue. The contract has a fixed ceiling value, of which approximately 73% has been consumed in only 62% of the contract period. At the current run rate, the remaining ceiling will be exhausted by approximately the time of the target closing date, leaving approximately 16 months of unfunded contract period before the contract's expiration. If the ceiling is not increased via a contract modification, revenue from this customer could decline by approximately half in FY2025 — a headwind of approximately 9–10% of total revenue. This risk is not disclosed in the CIM's investment highlights.

**Action:** Obtain written confirmation from the government Contracting Officer regarding ceiling modification prospects. Adjust the purchase price to reflect the risk of revenue decline. Consider a revenue-based earnout tied to contract continuation.

---

### RF-03 | HIGH | Reported EBITDA Declined Significantly Year-Over-Year; Recurring Items

The QoE data package reveals that Reported EBITDA declined approximately 27% year-over-year in FY2024 — a trend invisible to a reader relying solely on the CIM, which does not present historical Reported EBITDA for prior years. The adjustments required to reach Adjusted EBITDA represent approximately 24–29% of Reported EBITDA. Several adjustment categories recur across multiple years, calling into question their characterization as non-recurring.

**Action:** Require the quality of earnings advisor to independently validate each adjustment category and assess recurrence risk.

---

### RF-04 | HIGH | Owner Compensation Normalization — Potentially Illusory Add-Back

The EBITDA bridge includes an add-back for owner compensation normalization, reducing the CEO and COO base salaries to a market rate. However, both founders' salaries are contractually fixed at their current levels and they are expected to remain post-closing. Achieving this add-back requires renegotiating their employment agreements, which would trigger single-trigger change-of-control severance provisions — making the net economics of the normalization potentially negative.

**Action:** Clarify post-closing compensation arrangements before signing. If founders remain at current salary levels, eliminate the normalization add-back from the valuation basis.

---

### RF-05 | HIGH | Litigation Settlement Add-Back — Misidentified in QoE Data Package

The QoE EBITDA Bridge tab misidentifies the source of a litigation settlement add-back. The Litigation and Regulatory Summary reveals that the settlement relates to a previously resolved matter in which the Company received a payment — not a matter in which the Company made a payment. Adding back a one-time income receipt is an unusual and aggressive adjustment that inflates Adjusted EBITDA.

**Action:** Require the QoE report to clearly identify whether each adjustment is an expense add-back or an income exclusion, and confirm the source of the settlement item.

---

### RF-06 | HIGH | Fleet Age and Deferred Capital Expenditure Backlog

The fleet and equipment schedule reveals that approximately 45% of assets are at or past their stated useful life, with an average fleet age exceeding the weighted average useful life. The estimated replacement cost for past-useful-life units is approximately $9.8 million. Spreading this backlog over three years implies approximately $3.3 million per year in catch-up capex above the current run rate, suggesting true normalized maintenance capex is nearly double management's stated estimate.

**Action:** Commission an independent fleet condition assessment. Adjust the valuation model to reflect true normalized maintenance capex. Negotiate a purchase price reduction or escrow holdback to fund deferred fleet replacement.

---

### RF-07 | MEDIUM | Revenue Concentration and Government Contract Dependency

The top five customers represent approximately 50% of TTM revenue and the top ten represent approximately 67%. Government customers represent approximately 36% of revenue. Eight of the top twenty customer contracts contain assignment, change-of-control, or notification provisions. Twelve of the top twenty contracts include termination for convenience clauses with sixty days' notice or less.

**Action:** Map all change-of-control notification and consent requirements across the top twenty contracts before closing.
