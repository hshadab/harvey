## Executive Summary

This memorandum summarizes material red flags and diligence concerns identified across thirteen data room documents reviewed in connection with the proposed acquisition of Ridgeline Environmental Services, Inc. ("RES" or the "Company") by Sycamore Capital Partners, LP ("Buyer"). The proposed transaction is structured as a 100% stock acquisition at an indicative enterprise value of $182–$205 million (8.0x–9.0x FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA of $22.8M per the CIM).

Our review has identified **twenty-two (22) discrete red flags** spanning six risk categories: (1) Financial / Quality of Earnings, (2) Environmental & Regulatory, (3) Contractual & Customer, (4) Real Property & Leases, (5) Labor & Employment, and (6) Debt & Capital Structure. Several of these issues are individually material and, in combination, could warrant a meaningful reduction to the purchase price, enhanced indemnification protections, or restructuring of the transaction.

**The three most critical issues requiring immediate attention are:**

1. **DOE IDIQ Ceiling Exhaustion** — The Company's largest customer (19.2% of revenue) is projected to exhaust its contract ceiling by April–May 2025, creating a potential $12.1M+ revenue headwind in FY2025 that is not reflected in the valuation.
2. **EBITDA Overstatement / QoE Discrepancy** — Management's Adjusted EBITDA of $22.8M (CIM) conflicts with the QoE data room figure of $23.8M, and the $5.4M aggregate add-back package contains items that are recurring in nature, inflating the valuation basis by an estimated $2.5–$3.5M.
3. **Salt Lake City Lease / Permit Dual Default Risk** — The Company's second-largest facility cluster (~$16.2M revenue) operates under an unresolved lease assignment and an untransferred environmental permit, creating a potential landlord default and regulatory compliance exposure that could disrupt operations.

#### RF-01 | EBITDA Discrepancy Between CIM and QoE Data Package — HIGH SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** CIM (Section VI.B); QoE Data Request Response (EBITDA Bridge Tab)

**Finding:** The CIM presents FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA of **$22.8M**, while the QoE data package EBITDA Bridge tab shows Management Adjusted EBITDA of **$23.8M** — a $1.0M discrepancy. The QoE data package itself acknowledges this gap and states that "the specific adjustment category causing the discrepancy has not been identified." This unresolved reconciliation gap is a significant quality-of-earnings concern. The valuation is anchored to Adjusted EBITDA; a $1.0M discrepancy at an 8.0x–9.0x multiple implies a $8–9M swing in enterprise value.

**Recommended Action:** Require management to provide a fully reconciled EBITDA bridge with line-item support before finalizing the purchase price. The QoE firm (Bridgewater Forsyth Group) should resolve this discrepancy as a priority deliverable.

#### RF-02 | Aggressive / Recurring Nature of EBITDA Add-Backs — HIGH SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** CIM (Section VI.B); QoE Data Request Response (EBITDA Bridge Tab); Credit Agreement Summary (Section 4)

**Finding:** The $5.4M aggregate add-back package (per QoE data) includes items that are arguably recurring or that have appeared across multiple fiscal years:

- **Acquisition & Integration Costs ($800K in FY2024; $620K in FY2023; $1,240K in FY2022):** Integration costs have appeared in three consecutive years. The Pinnacle acquisition closed in March 2022; "substantially complete" integration in FY2024 is questionable given ongoing permit transfer issues and the unresolved SLC lease assignment. These costs appear semi-recurring.
- **ERP Implementation ($650K in FY2024; $385K in FY2023):** The QoE data notes Phase 2 is expected to complete in Q2 FY2025, meaning additional ERP costs will likely be incurred post-closing. This is not a clean one-time item.
- **Owner Compensation Normalization ($950K):** While a standard add-back, the Calloways' combined base salary of $1.75M is being normalized to $800K. Post-closing, if both founders remain (as contemplated), the buyer will need to negotiate new employment terms. The $950K add-back assumes replacement executives at $400K each — a figure that may understate market rates for a $127M revenue environmental services platform.
- **Asbestos Wind-Down ($1.8M):** This is a legitimate one-time item, but the service line generated losses in FY2023 ($686K negative contribution) and FY2024 wind-down costs of $1.8M. The CIM's characterization of this as purely non-recurring ignores the multi-year margin drag.
- **Litigation Settlement ($1.2M):** The QoE data labels this as the "Harper Environmental" settlement. However, the Litigation Summary (Section IV.A) clarifies this was the **Clearwater Logistics** settlement — a **receipt** of $1.2M, not a payment. Adding back a cash receipt as a non-recurring item inflates EBITDA. This add-back should be scrutinized: if it is a one-time receipt, it should be removed from EBITDA, not added back.

**Recommended Action:** Buyer's QoE team should challenge each add-back category. Estimated sustainable Adjusted EBITDA, after removing questionable items, may be $19.5–$21.0M, implying a valuation of $156–$189M at 8.0x–9.0x — materially below the $182–$205M indicative range.

#### RF-03 | Bank EBITDA vs. Management EBITDA Divergence — MEDIUM SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** Credit Agreement Summary (Section 4); CIM (Section VI.B)

**Finding:** The Credit Agreement defines "Consolidated EBITDA" with a cap of $500K on non-recurring add-backs. Under the bank definition, Consolidated EBITDA for FY2024 is approximately **$18.4M** — $4.4M below management's $22.8M figure. The bank's leverage ratio is therefore 2.10x ($38.7M / $18.4M), not the 1.70x implied by management's adjusted figure. This divergence is material for two reasons: (1) it confirms that a significant portion of the add-back package is not independently validated; and (2) it means the Company's true leverage profile is higher than presented.

**Recommended Action:** Use the bank's $18.4M Consolidated EBITDA as a cross-check on management's adjustments. Require Bridgewater Forsyth to independently validate each add-back against the bank definition.

#### RF-04 | Reported EBITDA Decline in FY2024 — MEDIUM SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** QoE Data Request Response (Historical P&L Tab)

**Finding:** The QoE P&L data shows FY2024 Reported EBITDA of **$26.0M** (including D&A add-back), but management presents Reported EBITDA of **$18.4M** as the bridge starting point — a $7.6M difference attributable to D&A treatment. More importantly, Reported EBITDA (per management's presentation) declined **27.0% year-over-year** from FY2023 ($25.2M) to FY2024 ($18.4M), while revenue grew 11.1%. This is a significant margin compression signal. The decline is partially explained by the $5.4M in add-backs, but even on an adjusted basis, the FY2024 Adjusted EBITDA margin of 18.7% (per QoE) is below FY2023's 24.5% adjusted margin — a 580 basis point compression. The CIM does not present historical Adjusted EBITDA for prior years, making trend analysis difficult.

**Recommended Action:** Require management to provide a full multi-year Adjusted EBITDA bridge (FY2020–FY2024) to assess whether margin compression is structural or temporary.

#### RF-05 | Fleet Replacement Backlog — Understated Maintenance CapEx — MEDIUM SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** QoE Data Request Response (Fleet & Equipment Schedule; CapEx Detail Tab)

**Finding:** The Fleet & Equipment Schedule reveals that **38 of 85 fleet assets (44.7%) are at or past their stated useful life**, with an average fleet age of 8.2 years against a 7.0-year weighted average useful life. The estimated replacement cost for past-useful-life units is **$9.8M**. Management's normalized maintenance CapEx estimate of $4.0–$4.5M per year appears understated given this backlog. Spreading the $9.8M replacement backlog over three years implies approximately $3.3M per year in catch-up CapEx above the current run-rate, suggesting true normalized maintenance CapEx of $7.3–$7.8M annually — nearly double management's estimate. This has a direct impact on free cash flow and the implied valuation.

**Recommended Action:** Conduct an independent fleet condition assessment. Adjust the CapEx model to reflect the deferred replacement backlog. Reduce free cash flow projections accordingly.

#### RF-07 | Grand Junction Facility — Stale Phase II ESA, Unquantified Remediation Exposure — HIGH SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** Phase II ESA Executive Summary; Environmental Permit Schedule (Section 2.1); Litigation Summary (Section VI.A); Real Property Lease Schedule (Section 2.1)

**Finding:** The Phase II ESA was completed in **June 2019** — over five years ago. The ESA identified:
- Petroleum hydrocarbon contamination (TPH-diesel up to 4,200 mg/kg; benzene in groundwater at 12 µg/L, exceeding the 5 µg/L Colorado standard)
- Chlorinated solvent contamination (PCE up to 18 mg/kg in soil; PCE at 22 µg/L and TCE at 8.7 µg/L in groundwater, both exceeding Colorado standards)
- Potential vapor intrusion pathway into the occupied 28,000 sq. ft. vehicle maintenance building (PCE at 450 µg/m³, exceeding EPA RSL of 240 µg/m³)
- Potential off-site plume migration — no off-site sentinel wells installed

The 2019 cost estimate of **$1.8–$2.6M** is now five years stale and explicitly noted by Elkhorn as requiring update before any transaction. The Company has accrued only $2.2M for Grand Junction remediation. Critically:
- **No updated Phase II ESA has been commissioned** despite the ESA's explicit recommendation to update before any transaction.
- **Vapor intrusion assessment has not been conducted**, leaving occupational health risk to employees unquantified.
- **Off-site migration has not been confirmed or ruled out.** If off-site migration is confirmed, remediation costs could increase materially and additional regulatory notification requirements under Colorado Regulation No. 41 would be triggered.
- The contamination is characterized as **gradual releases** — meaning the Pollution Legal Liability policy's **gradual contamination exclusion applies**, leaving this exposure uninsured.
- The ESA recommends DNAPL (dense non-aqueous phase liquid) assessment for chlorinated solvents, which could substantially increase remediation scope and cost.

**Recommended Action:** Commission an updated Phase II ESA and vapor intrusion assessment immediately. Obtain independent remediation cost estimate. Negotiate a specific environmental indemnity with a separate escrow (above the general 10% holdback) sized to cover the upper end of the updated cost range plus contingency. The LOI already contemplates special indemnification treatment for environmental matters.

#### RF-08 | CDPHE Enforcement Action — Active RCRA Violation — HIGH SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** Litigation Summary (Section III.A); Environmental Permit Schedule (Section 2.1); LOI (Section 5.5)

**Finding:** CDPHE issued a Notice of Violation (Docket No. CDPHE-HW-2024-0093) on July 18, 2024, alleging improper storage of approximately 14,000 gallons of F001/F002 listed hazardous waste at the Grand Junction facility. Key issues:
- **Penalty exposure:** Company's position = 12-day violation = max $450K; CDPHE's position = 31-day violation = max $1,162,500. Settlement range per management: $200K–$450K.

- Under the Credit Agreement, a material environmental event or governmental order requiring remediation in excess of $1M constitutes an **Event of Default**. While the current penalty estimate is below $1M, CDPHE's maximum position of $1.16M approaches this threshold.
- The LOI specifically calls out this matter for special indemnification treatment.

**Recommended Action:** Require resolution of the CDPHE enforcement action before closing, or negotiate a specific indemnity with a separate escrow. Obtain CDPHE's current negotiating position. Assess impact on the 2026 RCRA permit renewal.

#### RF-10 | Salt Lake City Permit Not Transferred — Operating Under Subsidiary's Permit — HIGH SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** Environmental Permit Schedule (Section 2.2); Litigation Summary (Section III.C); Real Property Lease Schedule (Section 4.1)

**Finding:** Utah Hazardous Waste Transfer Station Permit UTH-0441 for the Salt Lake City facility remains in the name of **Pinnacle Waste Solutions, LLC** — not RES — more than 2.5 years after the Pinnacle acquisition closed in March 2022. The permit transfer application has been pending with the Utah Division of Waste Management since 2022 and remains incomplete. The facility is operated by RES personnel under Pinnacle's permit. Under Utah Administrative Code R315-3, permit modifications reflecting changes in ownership require Division approval before becoming effective. This creates:
- A potential regulatory compliance issue if the Division determines that RES is operating without proper authorization.
- A risk that the proposed Sycamore acquisition could trigger additional scrutiny of the pending transfer application.
- Compounded risk with the unresolved SLC lease assignment (see RF-11).

**Recommended Action:** Require completion of the permit transfer as a pre-closing condition. Engage directly with the Utah Division of Waste Management to assess the status and timeline. Evaluate whether the proposed transaction will require a new permit application rather than a transfer.

#### RF-11 | Asbestos Long-Tail Liability — Uninsured Pre-2015 Exposure — MEDIUM SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** Litigation Summary (Section VI.B); Insurance Program Summary (Section 4); QoE Data Request Response (Service Line Detail Tab)

**Finding:** RES operated an asbestos abatement service line from 2012 through September 30, 2023, generating approximately $31.2M in cumulative revenue across ~145 commercial and industrial sites in CO, UT, WY, and MT. The current Pollution Legal Liability policy has a **retroactive date of January 1, 2015**, meaning work performed from 2012–2014 is **not covered** by the current policy. Additionally:
- The CGL policy contains a **blanket asbestos exclusion** with no tail coverage for completed operations.
- Two workers' compensation claims from former asbestos abatement employees remain open (filed 2021 and 2022), alleging occupational respiratory exposure.
- No reserve has been established for potential future asbestos-related personal injury claims.
- The QoE data confirms the service line generated losses in FY2023 and FY2024 wind-down costs of $1.8M — suggesting the decision to discontinue was driven by deteriorating economics, not purely strategic choice.

**Recommended Action:** Obtain a specific asbestos indemnity from sellers with a longer survival period (minimum 10 years). Evaluate procurement of an asbestos-specific tail policy. Conduct a site-by-site review of the 145 abatement projects for high-risk exposures.

#### RF-12 | DOE IDIQ Ceiling Exhaustion — Imminent Revenue Cliff — CRITICAL SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** Customer Contract Summary (DOE IDIQ Utilization Analysis Tab; Customer Concentration Summary Tab); CIM (Section VI.D); QoE Data Request Response (Revenue by Customer Tab)

**Finding:** This is the single most significant near-term revenue risk in the transaction. The DOE IDIQ contract (DE-EM-0005523) represents **19.2% of TTM revenue ($24.4M)**. Key facts:
- **Remaining ceiling:** Only $12.3M of the $45.0M ceiling remains available for new task orders.
- **Current run rate:** ~$24.4M/year (~$2.03M/month).
- **Projected ceiling exhaustion:** April–May 2025 — approximately at or before the target closing date.
- **Contract expiration:** August 31, 2026 — meaning there are approximately 16 months of unfunded contract period after ceiling exhaustion.
- **Revenue at risk:** If the ceiling is not increased, FY2025 DOE revenue could decline from $24.4M to ≤$12.3M — a **$12.1M revenue headwind** representing 9.5% of current total revenue.
- **Anti-Assignment Act:** The contract is non-assignable without Contracting Officer consent. While a stock acquisition may not technically constitute an assignment, the contract requires notification within 30 days of any change in ownership exceeding 50%, and failure to notify may be grounds for termination for default.
- The CIM presents the DOE relationship as a "cornerstone" of the business without disclosing the ceiling exhaustion risk.

**Recommended Action:** Immediately engage with the DOE Contracting Officer regarding: (1) ceiling modification or new contract award; (2) novation requirements for the proposed transaction; and (3) the notification obligation under Section H, Clause H-12. Adjust the valuation model to reflect a conservative DOE revenue scenario of $12.3M for FY2025. Consider a purchase price adjustment mechanism tied to DOE contract renewal/ceiling modification.

#### RF-19 | NLRB Unionization Petition — 62 Field Technicians — HIGH SEVERITY

**Recommended Action:** Obtain a full briefing from management and labor counsel on the status of the NLRB petition, the Company's response strategy, and the election timeline. Assess the financial impact of potential unionization on labor costs and operational flexibility. Consider whether the NLRB petition constitutes a material adverse change.

#### RF-22 | Change-of-Control Mandatory Prepayment — $39.2M+ Required at Closing — HIGH SEVERITY

**Source Documents:** Credit Agreement Summary (Sections 8 and 9); LOI (Section 4(b))

**Finding:** The Credit Agreement with High Plains National Bank contains a **mandatory prepayment upon Change of Control** provision requiring full repayment of all outstanding obligations within **five (5) business days** of the change of control event. The proposed 100% stock acquisition by Sycamore Capital Partners triggers the Change of Control definition under clauses (a) and (c) of the Credit Agreement. Key facts:
- **Total mandatory prepayment:** Approximately $39.2M, comprising $12.4M (revolver) + $26.3M (term loan) + $263K (1.0% prepayment premium on term loan, assuming closing in Year 3) + accrued interest.
- **No waiver mechanism:** The Credit Agreement expressly provides no mechanism to waive or obtain consent to a Change of Control in lieu of prepayment.
- **Derek Calloway key-man provision:** The Credit Agreement also defines Derek Calloway's departure as CEO (without a lender-approved successor) as an Event of Default. If the Calloways' post-closing roles change, this provision must be addressed.
